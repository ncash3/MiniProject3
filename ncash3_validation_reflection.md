# Validation Reflection — ncash3

Across the ten projects validated from cdamron3, 33 of 50 field comparisons matched before discussion (66.0%) and 47 matched after (94.0%). Agreement was perfect for Currentstatus and WhyRecovered, high for HasRecovered, and lowest for ActivityPattern (30.0% before discussion) and RecentThemes (20.0%). Three disagreements were left open after discussion: ActivityPattern for mirnylab/cooler and xeokit/xeokit-sdk, and RecentThemes for stan-dev/cmdstanpy.

The commits around the longest gaps showed some patterns. Gaps often came after stabilization or release work, such as cooler's version bump and changelog, bigstatsr's CRAN fixes, and intern's v1.4.1. Recovery came either from the original maintainer returning (intern, cooler, bigstatsr, MFrontGenericInterfaceSupport) or from a new contributor (Josh in hnn, Robert Aboukhalil in Ribbon, Jessica Flowers in crank). Outside changes also triggered work, such as the Julia ecosystem moves behind rveltz's dependency swap. A gap did not mean the project was dead: eight projects had a gap and came back, two had no gap at all, and every project counts as Active under the 2025-04-01 cutoff. Even so, hnn's most recent commit adds a README deprecation notice, so Active may overstate its health.

The main pitfalls were terminology and judgment calls. N/A versus No for projects without a gap was a wording difference that the Coding Guide settled. ActivityPattern labels depend on the time scale you look at, so irregular, declining and U-shaped can all describe the same series, and the Ribbon and cooler cases show that. RecentThemes depended on how merge, bot and refactor commits were counted, and on whether the commit list came from GitHub or WoC. Discussing each case against the Coding Guide and the yearly counts resolved most of them and showed which ones were real differences in interpretation.
